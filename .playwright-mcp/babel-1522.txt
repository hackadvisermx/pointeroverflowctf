Babel Correspondence Reference Sheet
=====================================

A nonce has been wrapped through five operations, applied in
some order. Four of the five are base-N encodings; the fifth
is a transposition cipher. Each is described below by its
characteristics. The player who recognises each operation,
and inverts them in the reverse of their application order,
recovers the nonce.

The five operations:

  "The ternary clerk."
    Three symbols will do: '0', '1', '2'. The underlying
    integer is written in that base.

  "The coin's purse."
    An alphanumeric alphabet of 58 characters, no visual
    twins (0, I, O, l are omitted). Bitcoin's.

  "The RFC's letter."
    Of the printable-ASCII base-85 variants, the one whose
    alphabet admits braces, backticks, and angle brackets.
    RFC 1924.

  "The vowel's cadence."
    Seven uppercase letters: the five English vowels in their
    standard phonetic order (A, E, I, O, U), then the
    semi-vowel Y, then the consonant X. Treated as the digits
    0..6 in that order.

  "The Zodiac's trick."
    A string is written across a grid of fixed width 7, then
    read down column by column. If the string's length isn't
    an even multiple of 7, the final row is padded on the
    right with '0' — the Arabic numeral deliberately absent
    from the base-58 alphabet — before the columns are read.
    (So after inverting this cipher, any trailing '0' chars
    are unambiguously padding, and get stripped.)

Determine the order of application from the character set at
each stage; invert; and when you've inverted all five, the
innermost value is your team's nonce (16 hexadecimal chars).
